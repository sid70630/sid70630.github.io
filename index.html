01 / About

Experiments tell a story. Analysis makes it readable.

My work asks how neutron-rich nuclei behave when the balance of protons and neutrons moves far from stability.

At the University of Brighton, my PhD centres on hafnium and tantalum isotopes studied at KISS, RIKEN. I combine measured decay and timing data with nuclear-structure models to identify excited states and understand how they decay.

01 — MEASURECapture the decay

Use detector signals, γ-ray energies and time correlations from experiments.

02 — ANALYSEFind the pattern

Build spectra, fit peaks and half-lives, and test coincidence relationships.

03 — INTERPRETExplain the state

Compare observations with Nilsson configurations and neighbouring nuclei.

Research highlight

An isomeric state in ¹⁸³Ta

My first-author work reports evidence for a π1/2⁺[411] isomeric state in neutron-rich tantalum-183.

The delayed decay was identified through β–γ timing and spectroscopy following the decay of ¹⁸³Hf. Its measured half-life offers a new constraint on the low-lying structure of ¹⁸³Ta.
